# Results & Analysis Summary

## 1. Interpolation Performance
The LOOCV process determined that Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) provided the most accurate reconstruction of the FUTO topography compared to baseline methods.

*   **IDW (Selected):** RMSE = 14.2 m
*   **Linear Interpolation:** RMSE = 16.8 m
*   **Nearest Neighbor:** RMSE = 21.3 m

## 2. Terrain Classification
The generated 150×150 grid (ranging from 159 m to 229 m in elevation) was segmented into three primary terrain classes. The distribution is highly balanced across the study area:
*   **Lowland:** ~33% of total area
*   **Midland:** ~34% of total area
*   **Highland:** ~33% of total area

## 3. Vulnerability Distribution
Based on the weighted hazard models, the FUTO campus and surrounding grid cells exhibit the following risk profiles:

### Flood Risk
*   **High Risk:** ~33% of grid cells (Predominantly Lowland areas like Avu and Eziobodo communities).
*   **Moderate Risk:** ~33% of grid cells.
*   **Low Risk:** ~34% of grid cells.

### Erosion Risk
*   **High Erosion Potential:** ~33% of grid cells (Correlating heavily with steep transitional slopes).
*   **Moderate Erosion Potential:** ~33% of grid cells.
*   **Low Erosion Potential:** ~34% of grid cells.

## 4. Conclusion
The FUTO campus exhibits significant topographical variation within a compact footprint. The models indicate that future infrastructural expansion must prioritize the Highland and specific Midland zones (approx. 34% of the analyzed area) that register low susceptibility to both inundation and soil degradation.
